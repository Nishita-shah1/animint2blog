For future developers

Come to learn. Stay for the work that outlives GSoC.

If you want to contribute to Animint and Google Summer of Code, start here. The motive is learning. A GSoC summer is one season. The habits you build, the people you meet, and the code you understand last much longer than that.

You will learn. That is the point. Not a scoreboard of pull requests, not a badge at the end of August. Learning how a real visualization library is built, reviewed, tested, and taught to the next person.

What you actually learn

The stack in public

Animint2 is R, JavaScript, D3, tests, and sometimes C++. You learn by reading a compiler that turns a ggplot into an interactive page, then changing one small piece without breaking hover, click, or CI.

Review as the classroom

Mentors will ask why a fallback exists, why a test clicks a hole, why a Fast curve is slow. That conversation is the curriculum. GSoC is the excuse to have it every week.

Tests before the story

A failing test that names the bug is stronger than a long explanation. You will learn to show the problem first, then the fix. That skill travels to every project after this one.

Beyond the summer

When GSoC ends, Animint still needs people who care about holes in polygons, compile time, coverage, and writing for users. The project is a community. Learning is how you belong in it.

The motive is learning

Apply to GSoC if you want a structured summer with mentors. Contribute to Animint even if you never apply. Clone the repo. Pick a small issue. Read someone else’s journal. Ask a question that shows you tried. The goal is not to “win” a slot. The goal is to leave knowing more than you did in May: how open source feels, how review works, and how to keep going in September.

This journal is one path through that. It is not a template you have to copy. It is proof that confusion, CI failures, and quiet weeks are part of learning too.
